The Cart on the Wrong Side

Once upon a time, in a long and brightly lit aisle of an American grocery store, a man in a green shirt pushed his cart with quiet confidence… on the left side.

Directly in his path (and looking extremely displeased about it) stood Grumblegus the Elder, Self-Appointed Sheriff of Aisle Etiquette and Defender of the Sacred Right-Hand Rule.

“Where’d you learn to push a cart?!” bellowed Grumblegus, face redder than the sirloin in the meat case. “Australia?!”

The man in green kept rolling forward, unbothered. “Just felt natural.”

But Grumblegus would not stand for such foreign heresy. In his kingdom (the frozen-food section between 2 and 4 p.m.), carts, like cars and common courtesy, kept strictly to the right. Anything else was pure foreign chaos.

Thus began the Great Cart Confrontation.

Wheels screeched.

A bag of frozen peas became collateral damage.

The store manager appeared, saw the frozen peas on the floor, sighed the sigh of a thousand overtime hours, and made them both reverse their carts like scolded pack animals.

And the moral of the story is:

Two rights may get one scolded.
