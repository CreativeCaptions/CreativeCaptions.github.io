The Newport Mysteries - The Unwritten Rule

Chapter 1 - Old Friends, New Memories

The Orange County Orcas were playing the Texas Outlanders on a warm Saturday afternoon, and James Newport had to admit that the game was almost as entertaining as the conversation going on beside him.

Frank Newport and Detective Bill West had met up again after their adventure at the Whale Festival, and the two old friends seemed to have a lot of catching up to do. James sat on Frank's other side, listening as the two men traded stories about the years when Frank had been a newspaper reporter and Bill had been a regular policeman working the streets.

"I still remember the first time we worked on a story together," Frank said, leaning back in his seat. "Although I suppose you wouldn't call it working together."

Bill laughed. "I wouldn't. You were a reporter who had a habit of showing up where you weren't wanted."

"I was gathering information," Frank said.

"You were asking questions after I told you to stop asking questions," Bill replied.

James smiled. "That sounds more like Dad."

Frank gave him a look. "I was a reporter, James. Asking questions was my job."

"And apparently ignoring police instructions was part of it," Bill added.

"Only when the police weren't asking the right questions," Frank said.

Bill shook his head, laughing. "See what I mean? He hasn't changed."

James looked from one man to the other. "So how did you two actually become friends?"

Frank pointed toward Bill. "Tell him."

"No, you tell him. You're the one who remembers everything," Bill said.

"That's because I wrote it down," Frank replied.

Bill chuckled. "That's not the same thing as remembering."

Frank started explaining how they had first crossed paths on a case involving a break-in at a local business. Bill had been one of the officers assigned to the investigation, while Frank had been covering the story for the newspaper. At first, neither man had been particularly impressed with the other.

"You thought I was getting in the way," Frank told Bill.

"You had a talent for appearing at exactly the wrong time," Bill said.

"And you thought reporters didn't belong anywhere near a police investigation," Frank said.

"I still think most reporters don't," Bill replied.

Frank grinned. "But I was different."

"You were stubborn," Bill said.

James laughed. "That sounds about right."

Bill nodded toward James. "Your father had a way of getting people to talk. Sometimes that was useful. Other times it was just annoying."

"And you kept coming back to me for information," Frank said.

"Because you knew things," Bill replied.

Frank smiled. "And that's when you realized I could be useful."

Bill smiled back. "Useful and annoying. Sometimes at the same time."

James looked interested. "So what was the first rule of working with Frank Newport?"

Bill glanced at Frank.

"Never tell Frank something unless you're prepared to see it in tomorrow's newspaper," Bill said.

Frank laughed. "That's not true."

Bill raised an eyebrow. "You want me to tell him about the mayor?"

Frank's smile disappeared.

"Absolutely not," Frank said.

James laughed as the two men continued arguing, neither noticing that the game had just ended the inning. For the moment, James was enjoying hearing about the younger versions of the two men sitting beside him.

He had no idea that before the afternoon was over, another investigation would give the three of them a new story to remember.

Chapter 2 - The Unwritten Rule

The game had reached the top of the ninth inning, and there wasn't much left to decide. The Orange County Orcas were leading the Texas Outlanders 11–0, with two outs and nobody on base.

James was still listening to the game on the Orcas app while watching the action on the field. The two radio announcers were keeping up a steady conversation, talking about the big lead and the fact that most of the fans were already starting to gather their things.

"Well, it looks like the Orcas are going to make this one easy," one announcer said.

"Easy for Orange County, anyway," the other announcer replied. "The Outlanders haven't had much going their way today."

James glanced toward the field as a new batter stepped into the box.

"Who's that?" Frank asked.

"Gunner Rawls," James said. "He's the next batter."

Bill watched Rawls take a few practice swings. "I've heard of him."

"Good player," Frank said.

"Good player with a bad attitude," Bill replied.

Rawls had a reputation for being difficult, and from the way he looked around the field, he seemed determined to prove it.

The first pitch came in, and Rawls watched it go by.

"Strike one," the radio announcer called.

Rawls stepped out of the batter's box and stared toward the Orcas dugout.

The next pitch came.

Rawls suddenly dropped the bat down and pushed the ball toward the third-base line.

"Bunt!" the first announcer shouted.

The ball rolled slowly toward the charging third baseman. Rawls took off, and despite the easy grounder, he beat the throw to first.

"Bunt single!" the second announcer said. "And that gives Gunner Rawls a base hit."

The first announcer sounded surprised. "That's an interesting choice. You've got two outs in the ninth, your team is down eleven to nothing, and you've just broken up what would have been a no-hitter with a bunt."

"There's an unwritten rule in baseball about that," the second announcer replied. "You don't break up a no-hitter that way. If you're going to get a hit, you swing the bat."

"Exactly," the first announcer said. "A bunt for a base hit in that situation is generally considered a slap in the face to the pitcher."

James looked at Frank. "So Rawls knew exactly what he was doing."

Frank nodded. "Sounds like it."

Bill kept his eyes on Rawls. "And if he has the attitude Frank says he does, I'm guessing that's exactly why he did it."

Rawls looked toward the Orcas dugout.

Then he yelled, "Orcs!"

James lowered his phone.

"What did he just say?" Frank asked.

"Orcs," James replied.

Bill frowned. "That's not exactly a friendly nickname."

Everyone in the stadium knew what Rawls meant. Orcs were ugly, violent creatures from fantasy stories, and the word was clearly being used as an insult toward the Orcas players.

Rawls kept shouting toward the dugout.

The response was immediate.

Players poured out of the Orcas dugout, and the Outlanders came running from their side of the field. Within seconds, players from both teams were pushing and shoving near first base.

"Here we go," Bill said.

The three men watched as coaches and other players tried to separate everyone. One Orcas player appeared to say something to Rawls, who answered with another angry shout.

The umpires finally managed to pull the players apart. Rawls was pointed toward the clubhouse, and he didn't look happy about it.

"He's been thrown out," the radio announcer said. "Gunner Rawls is gone from the game."

A pinch-runner took Rawls's place at first, but it didn't matter. The next batter grounded out, ending the game.

The crowd began heading toward the exits, but James, Frank and Bill remained in their seats.

"We might as well wait," Frank said. "There's no reason to get caught in that traffic."

Bill nodded. "Besides, I'd rather see what happens with Rawls."

James looked at him. "You think there's going to be more trouble?"

Bill watched the Outlanders disappear toward their clubhouse.

"With a guy like that, I wouldn't be surprised," Bill said.

Chapter 3 - A Deadly Discovery

The crowd was finally beginning to thin out when Bill's phone rang. He looked at the screen, answered, and listened without saying much.

James could tell from his expression that something had changed.

"What is it?" Frank asked.

Bill ended the call and slipped his phone back into his pocket.

"Gunner Rawls is dead," Bill said.

James stared at him. "Dead?"

"Apparently he was found in the visitors' locker room," Bill said. "I'm being asked to come over and take a look."

Frank stood up. "Well, I suppose our quiet afternoon at the ballpark is over."

Bill gave him a tired look. "You two don't have to come with me."

"We know," James said. "We're coming anyway."

Bill shook his head, but there was a small smile on his face. "I had a feeling you'd say that."

As they made their way toward the visitors' dugout, Bill stopped them before they entered the restricted area.

"You stay out of the way and don't touch anything," Bill said. "I'll make sure the officers know you're with me."

James nodded. "Understood."

Bill looked at Frank. "And that goes for you, too."

Frank put a hand on his chest. "Bill, I'm offended that you would even suggest otherwise."

"Frank, I've known you too long to believe that," Bill replied.

They followed Bill into the visitors' area, where several police officers were already working around the locker room. The mood was completely different from the excitement that had filled the stadium only minutes earlier.

Bill spoke with one of the officers who had been called to the scene.

"What happened?" Bill asked.

"One of the Outlanders' players found him," the officer explained. "Rawls was lying near the lockers. He wasn't breathing, and there was no response when they tried to wake him."

"Any idea what killed him?" Bill asked.

"Not yet," the officer replied. "It looks like he was hit on the side of the head with something heavy. There's a pretty serious injury there."

Bill looked toward the locker room. "Have you moved anything?"

"No," the officer said. "We're still processing the scene. We're trying to document everything before we start moving things around. The coroner is on the way."

Bill nodded. "Anybody see Rawls after the game?"

"We're still talking to people," the officer replied. "A lot of players were outside during the fight, then everyone scattered once the game ended. We're trying to establish who came back into the locker room afterward."

Bill continued asking questions while James and Frank stayed several feet away.

James slowly looked around the room.

The police were concentrating on the body and the immediate area around it, which made sense. James knew they had a job to do, but he also knew that sometimes small things could be overlooked when everyone was focused on the obvious.

Frank seemed to be thinking the same thing.

He moved slowly along one side of the room, looking without touching anything.

James did the same on the other side.

Neither man said much. They didn't want to attract attention or interfere with the investigation. They simply watched.

A towel was lying on the floor near one of the lockers. A pair of shoes had been kicked underneath a bench. Several baseball bags were scattered around the room.

The afternoon had started with a baseball game and an argument over an unwritten rule.

Now there was a dead man on the floor, and somewhere in that room might be the first clue to who had killed him.

Chapter 4 - The Bloody Bat

The coroner arrived a few minutes later, carrying a medical bag as two officers stepped aside to let him into the locker room. He introduced himself to Bill and then went over to where Gunner Rawls was lying on the floor.

James and Frank stayed back while the coroner examined the body. Bill stood nearby, watching and waiting for an initial assessment.

After a few minutes, the coroner carefully examined the injury on the side of Rawls's head.

"It looks like he was hit with something heavy," the coroner said. "Something with a fairly solid surface. From the shape of the injury, I'd say something like a baseball bat could have caused it."

James looked around the locker room.

"There's a lot of those around," James said.

Bill gave him a quick look but didn't have time to answer.

One of the officers approached carrying a baseball bat inside an evidence bag.

"Detective West, we found this in one of the players' lockers," the officer said.

Bill looked at the bag. There were dark stains along part of the bat.

"Blood?" Bill asked.

"Looks like it," the officer replied.

Bill took a closer look without touching the evidence bag.

"Whose locker?" Bill asked.

The officer pointed toward the row of lockers. "One of the Outlanders' players. We're still confirming whose it is."

James stepped a little closer.

"So the killer hit Rawls with the bat and then put it in somebody's locker?" James asked.

"That's what it looks like," Bill said.

Frank looked at the bat. "Wouldn't the killer have blood on himself?"

"Maybe," Bill replied. "But if the person was wearing a uniform or some kind of outer clothing, they might have had something covering their clothes. And if they were able to get rid of whatever they were wearing before leaving, that could explain it."

The officer nodded. "We're checking the lockers and talking to the players now."

Bill looked toward the body.

"Let's assume for the moment that's the murder weapon. Whoever did this hit Rawls here, then had to figure out what to do with the bat."

"Hide it in the locker," Frank said.

"Exactly," Bill replied. "The killer probably figured nobody would immediately notice a baseball bat in a baseball locker room."

James looked at the evidence bag again.

"But wouldn't that mean the killer knew whose locker it was?" James asked.

Bill paused.

"Not necessarily," Bill said. "They could have used any locker they thought would give them time to get away."

James nodded, but he wasn't completely satisfied.

Frank seemed to notice.

"Something bothering you?" Frank asked.

"I don't know," James said. "It just seems almost too convenient."

Bill heard him.

"That's why we don't decide anything yet," Bill said. "The bat may be the murder weapon, or it may be something somebody wants us to think is the murder weapon."

The coroner continued examining Rawls while the officers photographed the bat and the locker where it had been found.

Bill turned to James and Frank.

"And this is why I told you two not to touch anything," Bill said.

Frank smiled. "We haven't."

Bill looked at James.

James nodded. "We promise."

Bill turned back toward the locker room.

"Good. Because we've got a dead man, a bloody bat, and a locker room full of people who were angry at him less than an hour ago."

James and Frank stayed a little longer, but eventually Bill turned toward them.

"You two should probably head home," Bill said. "I'll be here for a while."

James nodded. "All right. Let us know if you need anything."

"I will," Bill said.

Frank looked toward the locker room. "Looks like you've got a long night ahead of you."

"Probably," Bill replied. "But I'll be fine."

James held out his hand. "We'll see you later, Bill."

Bill shook his hand. "Take care, James."

Frank gave Bill a brief hug. "Good luck, old friend."

"Thanks, Frank," Bill said.

James and Frank headed toward the exit, leaving Bill behind with the officers.

As they walked through the quieting stadium, James glanced back toward the visitors' area.

"That was quite a change from the afternoon we expected," James said.

Frank nodded. "It certainly was."

Chapter 5 - A Possible Motive

James and Frank met up at James's house. Jennifer was in the living room with Tim and Jill when they came through the door.

"Hi, Dad," Jennifer said.

"Hi, Jennifer," Frank replied. "How are you two doing?"

"Fine," Jill said.

Tim looked up from his phone. "I heard about Gunner Rawls."

Frank stopped and looked at him. "You already heard?"

"It's all over the sports news," Tim said. "They said he was found dead after the game. That's crazy."

"It certainly wasn't how I expected the afternoon to end," Frank said.

Tim shook his head. "Rawls was a great player, but he was always getting into trouble. Did you hear about the fight between the two teams?"

"We were at the ballpark when that happened," James said. "Frank, Bill and I were sitting in the stands watching the game."

"Really?" Tim asked.

James nodded. "We were there when Rawls came up to bat in the ninth inning. We saw him bunt for a hit and then start yelling at the Orcas dugout. That's what started the fight."

Tim shook his head. "That sounds like Rawls."

Frank smiled. "Apparently, he had quite a reputation."

Tim wanted to hear more, but Frank held up a hand.

"Give your grandfather a chance to sit down first," Frank said.

Tim laughed and went back to his phone while James and Frank sat down.

"I've been making a few calls," Frank said.

James looked at him. "About Rawls?"

"Of course. I couldn't just go home and forget about it," Frank replied.

"What did you find out?" James asked.

"Rawls had been having problems with his agent, Johnny Cage," Frank said.

James raised an eyebrow. "His agent?"

"Yes," Frank said. "Apparently, they had been fighting for some time. Cage was worried that Rawls's attitude was becoming a problem because he's trying to negotiate Rawls's next contract."

James nodded. "That makes sense. If a player keeps getting thrown out of games and picking fights, teams might think twice before offering him a big contract."

"That's exactly what Cage was worried about," Frank said. "He had been warning Rawls to straighten himself out."

"And Rawls listened?" James asked.

Frank laughed. "Apparently not. Things got so bad that Rawls was threatening to fire Cage."

James leaned back. "So his agent had a lot to lose if Rawls ruined the contract negotiations."

"That's what I was thinking," Frank said.

"Did you find out how serious the argument was?" James asked.

"Serious enough that other people knew about it. They weren't exactly keeping it quiet," Frank replied.

James glanced toward the kitchen, where Jennifer was talking with Jill.

"That's a pretty good motive," James said.

"It's a possible motive," Frank corrected. "We shouldn't get ahead of ourselves."

James smiled. "You're starting to sound like Bill."

"Bill has been rubbing off on me," Frank said.

Frank pulled out his phone.

"I think it's time we let Bill know what I found," Frank said.

He called Bill and waited for him to answer.

"West," Bill said when he picked up.

"Bill, it's Frank," Frank said. "I found something you should know about Rawls."

"What did you find?" Bill asked.

"Rawls was having serious problems with his agent, Johnny Cage. Cage was worried about Rawls's attitude and how it might affect his upcoming contract negotiations," Frank said.

"How bad were things between them?" Bill asked.

"Bad enough that Rawls was threatening to fire him," Frank replied. "Apparently, Cage had been warning him to straighten himself out, but Rawls wasn't interested in listening."

Bill was quiet for a moment.

"That's interesting," Bill said. "Very interesting."

"That's what I thought," Frank replied.

"Do you know where Cage is?" Bill asked.

"Not yet," Frank said.

"I do," Bill replied. "He's still in town."

Frank looked at James.

"Bill says he knows where Cage is," Frank told him.

Bill continued, "It's time we have a talk with Johnny Cage and find out exactly what was going on between him and Rawls."

"Sounds good to me," Frank said. "We'll meet you there."

"All right," Bill replied. "I'll see you soon."

Frank ended the call and put his phone away.

"Looks like we're going back to work," James said.

Frank smiled. "Apparently we are."

From the other room, Tim started laughing.

"Johnny Cage," he called out. "Sounds cagey to me."

James and Frank looked at each other and laughed.

Chapter 6 - The Former Ballplayer

Bill, James and Frank met up at Johnny Cage's office a short time later. The office was in a small professional building, and the three men walked through the front door and approached the receptionist's desk.

Bill showed his badge.

"Good afternoon. I'm Detective Bill West with the police department. This is James Newport, and this is Frank Newport. We need to speak with Johnny Cage."

The receptionist smiled politely. "He's in his office. You can go right in."

"Thank you," Bill said.

The three men walked down the short hallway and knocked on the open office door.

A man sitting behind a desk looked up.

"Johnny Cage?" Bill asked.

"Yes," Johnny replied.

"I'm Detective Bill West. This is James Newport and Frank Newport," Bill said.

Johnny stood and walked around his desk.

"Nice to meet you," Johnny said.

They shook hands.

"Have a seat," Johnny said.

Bill sat down while James and Frank took the other two chairs.

"We're here because of Gunner Rawls," Bill said. "We understand you and Mr. Rawls had been having some problems."

Johnny sighed. "Yes, we had some disagreements."

"Frank has been making some calls," Bill continued. "He tells me you were concerned that Gunner's behavior was going to hurt his contract negotiations."

"I was," Johnny said. "I kept telling Gunner that he needed to calm down. His talent wasn't the problem. His attitude was."

"And he threatened to fire you?" Bill asked.

Johnny nodded. "More than once."

James listened as Bill continued asking questions, but his attention began to wander around the office. There were framed photographs of baseball players on the walls, along with a few team pictures.

Then he noticed a baseball trophy sitting on a shelf.

James pointed toward it.

"Did you play baseball?" James asked.

Johnny looked over at the trophy and smiled.

"Yes. Minor League Baseball," Johnny said.

James looked interested. "Were you a pretty good hitter?"

"I was good enough to get close to the Majors," Johnny said. "I played several years, but I suffered a leg injury that ended my playing career."

"That must have been tough," James said.

"It was," Johnny admitted. "I'd spent most of my life playing baseball, and suddenly I couldn't do it anymore."

James nodded toward the trophy.

"I guess being an agent gives you a way to stay around the game."

Johnny smiled.

"That's one of the reasons I enjoy it," Johnny said. "I still get to work with players, go to games and be part of baseball. It's not exactly the same, but I really do enjoy what I do."

Bill stood.

"Thank you for your time, Mr. Cage. We'll let you get back to work," Bill said.

Johnny stood and shook Bill's hand.

"Of course. I hope you find whoever did this," Johnny said.

He shook hands with James and then Frank.

"Thanks for talking with us," James said.

"You're welcome," Johnny said.

The three men walked out of the office and headed toward the parking lot.

Once they were outside, James looked at Bill.

"Did you notice that trophy?" James asked.

Bill nodded. "The baseball trophy?"

"Yeah. Johnny Cage used to be a minor league hitter."

Frank looked at James. "Where are you going with this?"

"We already know Rawls was killed with a baseball bat," James said. "If Cage was a good enough hitter to almost make the Majors, he certainly knew how to swing one."

Bill nodded slowly. "So you're thinking he could have delivered a blow like that."

"Exactly," James said. "He had the baseball experience, and we already know he had a reason to be angry with Rawls."

Frank looked back toward the office.

"So now we've got motive and ability."

"But we still don't know if he had the opportunity," Bill said.

James nodded. "That's what we need to find out."

Chapter 7 - Caught on Camera

The next morning, Bill, James and Frank met at the police station. Bill had spent some time going over what they knew about Gunner Rawls and Johnny Cage.

"We know Cage admitted he talked to Rawls after the game," Bill said. "He says Rawls was alive when he left him."

James nodded. "So we need to know exactly when Cage went in and when he came out."

"That's what I was thinking," Bill said. "The stadium has security cameras covering the hallways and entrances. They don't have cameras inside the locker room, but they may show us something."

Frank leaned forward. "Have you gotten the footage?"

Bill nodded. "The stadium security department sent it over this morning."

He turned on a monitor and brought up the first recording.

The time stamp showed the top of the ninth inning.

"Here's Cage," Bill said.

The three men watched as Johnny Cage walked down the hallway toward the visitors' locker room. He was wearing a clean shirt, a pair of neatly pressed pants and a jacket. Nothing about him looked unusual.

Bill paused the video.

"Time stamp says 3:47 p.m."

He started the video again.

Cage opened the door and disappeared inside.

James watched the clock on the screen.

"How long was he in there?"

"We're about to find out," Bill said.

They watched several minutes of the recording. Then Cage came back through the door.

James leaned forward.

Cage's clothes no longer looked neat. His shirt was partly untucked, his jacket was crooked and his pants looked rumpled. There was also a dark stain on one leg of his pants.

"Stop it," James said.

Bill paused the recording.

Frank looked closely at the screen.

"Is that blood?"

"It sure looks like it," Bill said.

They watched the next few seconds.

Cage stood in the hallway and looked both ways. He appeared to be checking to see if anyone was watching him. Then he pulled at his jacket, straightened his shirt and tried to make himself look presentable.

"He's trying to clean himself up," James said.

"Or at least make himself look like nothing happened," Frank added.

Bill started the video again.

Cage walked quickly down the hallway and disappeared around a corner.

Bill brought up another camera.

"This one covers a side entrance."

The time stamp was several minutes later.

Cage appeared on the screen again.

He walked through the side entrance and looked around before stepping outside.

The bloodstain was still visible on his pants.

Bill stopped the video.

"Same clothes," Frank said.

"Same bloodstain," Bill replied.

James looked at the time stamp.

"So we know exactly when he went into the locker room and when he came out."

Bill nodded.

"And we know he went in looking perfectly normal and came out looking like he'd been in a fight."

James watched the frozen image of Cage on the screen.

"There's something else."

Bill looked at him.

"He looked around before he left. Then he straightened his clothes."

"Like he didn't want anyone to notice how he looked," Frank said.

Bill nodded.

"We already know Cage had a reason to be angry with Rawls. Now we've got him going into the locker room, coming out with blood on his pants, and then leaving through a side entrance."

James looked at Bill.

"That doesn't look good for Johnny Cage."

"No," Bill said. "It doesn't."

Frank looked at the screen again.

"But we still need to know whose blood that is."

Bill turned off the monitor.

"That's what we're going to find out."

Chapter 8 - The Bloodstained Pants

Later that morning, Bill, James and Frank arrived at Johnny Cage's house with several other police officers. Bill walked up to the front door and knocked.

After a moment, the door opened.

Johnny looked surprised to see them.

"Detective West. What can I do for you?" Johnny asked.

"Mr. Cage, we have a warrant to search your house," Bill said.

Johnny looked at the officers standing behind Bill.

"A warrant?" Johnny asked.

"Yes," Bill said. "We're going to search the house for evidence related to Gunner Rawls's murder."

Johnny shook his head. "You really think I killed Gunner?"

"We're not here to discuss what I think," Bill replied. "We're here to search your house."

Johnny stepped aside.

"Go ahead," Johnny said.

The officers entered and began checking the rooms. James and Frank remained near the front door with Bill while Johnny watched the officers move through his house.

"I don't understand what you're looking for," Johnny said.

"Evidence," Bill replied.

"Such as?" Johnny asked.

"Anything that might help us determine what happened to Gunner Rawls," Bill said.

Johnny gave him a tired look.

"I already told you I argued with Gunner. I also told you I left him alive," Johnny said.

"I remember," Bill said.

"So why are you treating me like I'm a murderer?" Johnny asked.

Bill looked at him.

"Because people who have something to hide don't always tell us everything," Bill said.

Johnny shook his head.

"I don't have anything to hide," Johnny said.

Frank glanced at James but didn't say anything.

The officers continued their search. A few minutes later, one of them came back carrying a pair of pants in an evidence bag.

"Detective West," the officer said.

Bill turned.

"What have you got?" Bill asked.

"We found these in a hamper upstairs. There's a bloodstain on one of the legs," the officer said.

Johnny's expression changed.

"Those are mine," Johnny said.

Bill looked at the pants, then back at Johnny.

"That's interesting," Bill said.

"What's interesting?" Johnny asked.

"We have security camera footage of you leaving the stadium after Rawls was killed," Bill said.

Johnny stared at him.

"So?" Johnny asked.

"So your clothes were rumpled, and there was a stain on your pants," Bill said.

Johnny didn't answer.

Bill continued, "The cameras show you looking around before you left. Then you straightened your clothes and hurried away through a side entrance."

"I was upset," Johnny said. "I had just argued with Gunner."

"Maybe," Bill said.

Bill held up the evidence bag.

"But you'd better hope this bloodstain doesn't match Gunner Rawls's blood."

Johnny looked at the pants.

"I don't know anything about that blood," Johnny said.

"That's what the laboratory is going to help us determine," Bill said.

Johnny looked at Bill.

"Am I under arrest?" Johnny asked.

"Not yet," Bill said. "We're taking you to the station while the blood is tested."

Johnny frowned.

"You can't just take me in because there's blood on a pair of my pants," Johnny said.

"We can when we have a warrant, camera footage showing you leaving the scene with a similar stain, and a dead man whose blood may be on those pants," Bill said.

Johnny looked toward James and Frank.

"You two believe this?" Johnny asked.

James didn't answer.

Frank shook his head slightly.

"We're trying to figure out what happened, just like Bill," Frank said.

Bill motioned toward the officers.

"Let's go," Bill said.

Johnny sighed and stepped outside with them.

As the officers led him toward the police car, James looked at Frank.

"If that blood is Rawls's, things don't look very good for Cage," James said.

Frank nodded.

"No, they don't," Frank said.

Bill looked back at them.

"That's why we're going to wait for the test results before we decide anything," Bill said.

James nodded.

For now, Johnny Cage was going to the police station, and the answer to one very important question was waiting at the laboratory.

Was the blood on those pants Gunner Rawls's?

Chapter 9 - The Confession

Down at the police station, Johnny Cage sat in an interrogation room with his lawyer beside him. Through the one-way window, Captain Weatherby, Detective Bill West, James Newport and Frank Newport watched them.

Johnny looked nervous as he sat at the table, while his lawyer remained calm and kept talking quietly to him.

"I still don't understand why we're here," Johnny said.

"Just let Detective West do his job," the lawyer replied. "Don't say anything unless I tell you to."

Outside the room, Bill kept his eyes on Johnny.

"He looks worried," James said.

"I would be worried too," Frank replied.

Before Bill could answer, a police officer approached them carrying a report.

"Detective West," the officer said.

Bill turned toward him.

"We got the results from the laboratory," the officer said.

Bill took the report.

"The blood from the pants matches the blood from Gunner Rawls," Bill said.

James looked at Frank.

"So that's it," James said.

Bill nodded.

"That's it," Bill said.

He looked through the window at Johnny Cage.

"Stay here," Bill said.

Bill walked into the interrogation room and closed the door behind him.

Johnny's lawyer stood.

"Detective, do you have something?" the lawyer asked.

Bill placed the report on the table.

"We do," Bill said.

He looked directly at Johnny.

"The blood found on your pants matches Gunner Rawls's blood," Bill said.

Johnny stared at him.

Bill continued, "The laboratory has confirmed that the blood on those pants came from Gunner Rawls."

His lawyer put a hand on his shoulder.

"Johnny, don't say anything. Let me handle this," the lawyer said.

Johnny was quiet for several seconds.

Then he looked up.

"No," Johnny said.

His lawyer frowned.

"Johnny?" the lawyer asked.

"I want to tell him what happened," Johnny said.

"Johnny, I strongly advise against that," the lawyer said.

"I know what you're saying," Johnny replied. "But I can't keep pretending."

Bill pulled out a chair and sat across from him.

"Then tell me what happened," Bill said.

Johnny took a deep breath.

"After Rawls's last at-bat, I went into the locker room to talk to him. We had been arguing about the contract, and I wanted to get one more chance to make him understand what he was doing to himself," Johnny said.

"What happened?" Bill asked.

"We started arguing. Then it turned into a fight," Johnny said.

Johnny looked down at the table.

"Rawls pushed me aside and called me a minor league player trying to live through major league players. Then he told me I was fired," Johnny said.

Bill remained quiet.

"I was furious," Johnny continued. "I had spent years trying to make it to the Majors. I had an injury that ended my playing career, and I built a new life as an agent. Then he stood there and threw that in my face."

"What happened next?" Bill asked.

Johnny closed his eyes for a moment.

"I saw a bat nearby. I grabbed it and said, 'I'll show you minor leaguers can hit too, you arrogant jerk,'" Johnny said.

Bill remained silent.

"I swung the bat and hit him on the side of the head," Johnny said.

Johnny's lawyer shook his head.

"Johnny, stop," the lawyer said.

But Johnny continued.

"I know what I did. I know it was wrong," Johnny said.

Bill looked at him.

"Did you know he was dead?" Bill asked.

"No," Johnny said.

Johnny shook his head.

"That's what I've been trying to tell you. Rawls was still alive when I left. He was on the floor, but he was breathing. I panicked. I thought someone else would find him and get him help."

"Why didn't you call for help?" Bill asked.

Johnny looked down.

"I was scared. I knew what I'd done, and I didn't want anyone to see me there," Johnny said.

Bill stood.

"So you left him," Bill said.

Johnny nodded.

"I did," Johnny said.

Bill picked up the report from the table.

"The blood on your pants, the security footage, and now your confession all tell us the same thing," Bill said.

Johnny said nothing.

Bill opened the door.

"Johnny Cage, you're under arrest for the murder of Gunner Rawls," Bill said.

The officers waiting outside entered the room and placed Johnny under arrest.

James watched as Johnny was led away.

Frank looked at him.

"Looks like we have our answer," Frank said.

James nodded.

"Yes, we do," James said.

Captain Weatherby turned toward James and Frank with a smile.

"Now I know why Bill likes having you two around," Weatherby said.

Frank smiled. "We've been trying to tell him that."

Bill shook his head.

"Don't encourage them, Captain," Bill said.

Weatherby laughed.

"Too late," Weatherby said.

Bill looked at James and Frank.

"Let's go home," Bill said.

For the first time since the game ended, James felt like the strange afternoon at the ballpark was finally coming to an end.

Chapter 10 - Another Day at the Ballpark

A few weeks later, the Newport family was back at the ballpark.

This time, there was no murder, no police investigation and, as far as James knew, nobody was planning to start a fight.

James sat beside Jennifer, while Frank and Margaret were seated nearby with Tim and Jill. Everyone was wearing an Orange County Orcas cap, and Jill was holding a stuffed Orcas that she had picked up at the stadium gift shop.

"I think Jill likes that thing more than she likes the game," Jennifer said.

Jill hugged the stuffed animal.

"It's cute," Jill said.

Tim laughed. "You're supposed to be watching baseball."

"I am watching baseball," Jill said.

"You've been talking to your Orcas for ten minutes," Tim said.

Jill looked down at the stuffed animal.

"He's watching too," Jill said.

Frank laughed.

"Well, at least he's paying attention," Frank said.

James smiled and looked out at the field. The game was close, and the crowd was enjoying every minute of it.

A familiar voice came from behind them.

"Newports!"

James turned around and saw Bill West walking toward them.

"Bill!" James said.

Bill smiled and stopped beside their seats.

"Good to see you all again," Bill said.

He greeted James and Jennifer, then nodded toward Frank and Margaret.

"How are you doing, Frank?" Bill asked.

"Doing fine, Bill. And you?" Frank asked.

"Can't complain," Bill said.

Bill looked at Jill and her stuffed Orcas.

"Looks like somebody came prepared," Bill said.

Jill held up the stuffed animal.

"His name is Orcy," Jill said.

Bill laughed.

"Good name," Bill said.

He looked around at all the Newport family members wearing their Orcas caps.

"You people really are becoming Orcas fans," Bill said.

James smiled.

"After everything that happened here, we figured we might as well," James said.

Bill pointed toward another section of the stadium.

"My family is sitting over there. I came over to say hello when I saw you," Bill said.

Frank nodded.

"How are things going?" Frank asked.

"Pretty good," Bill said. "It's nice not having to deal with a murder every time we come to a ballgame."

James laughed.

"I agree," James said.

Bill looked at him.

"Although I have to admit, you and Frank weren't much help in staying out of the investigation," Bill said.

"We were invited," Frank said.

Bill laughed.

"That's one way of putting it," Bill said.

He looked toward the field as the next batter stepped into the box.

"Well, I'd better get back to my family," Bill said.

"Good seeing you, Bill," James said.

"You too," Bill said.

Bill waved to everyone and headed back toward his family.

The Newports settled back into their seats and watched the game.

A few minutes later, the Orcas batter hit a long fly ball toward the seats.

"That's going!" Tim said.

Tim jumped to his feet.

The ball came toward their section, and Tim reached up just as it dropped into the crowd.

He came down with the baseball in his hand.

"I caught it!" Tim shouted.

Everyone around him cheered.

Frank laughed.

"Looks like we've got another Newport baseball story," Frank said.

Tim held the ball up proudly.

James smiled.

"This one doesn't involve a murder, though," James said.

Jennifer laughed.

"Let's keep it that way," Jennifer said.

Jill hugged her stuffed Orcas as the crowd continued cheering.

For the Newport family, it was just another day at the ballpark.

And that was exactly how they liked it.
