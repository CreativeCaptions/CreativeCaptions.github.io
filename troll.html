The Troll Who Took Plastic

Once upon a time (which is to say, last Tuesday), a motorist named Jack was driving his perfectly ordinary blue hatchback across the countryside when he came upon a stone bridge spanning a modest river. A wooden sign proclaimed TOLL BRIDGE. A red stop sign stood beside it. And blocking the way was a large, warty, green-skinned troll with matted fur, a single earring, and an expression of pure entrepreneurial glee.

Jack rolled down his window. “Good afternoon. How much to cross?”

The troll held up a sleek little payment terminal and grinned. “One gold digital piece. I accept Marketcard, TOLL, Bean Pay…”

Jack blinked. “No coins? No riddles? No threat to eat me if I guess wrong?”

The troll looked offended. “What do you think this is, the Dark Ages? Cash is inefficient. Riddles take too long. And eating travelers is a compliance nightmare—health inspectors, insurance, the whole nine yards. Tap, insert, or swipe. Your choice.”

Jack tapped his Marketcard.

“Transaction complete,” the troll said, stepping aside with a flourish. “Have a nice day.”

Jack drove across. Halfway over he glanced in the rearview mirror. The troll was already waving the terminal at the next car, cheerfully listing payment options once more.

And so Jack learned the true moral of the story:

No matter the times, the Troll must be paid.
